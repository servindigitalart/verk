# Verk — Phase 5 Report
> "El sitio ya funciona. Ahora debe convertirse en una experiencia."
> Full implementation report. Every section below states what changed, why the
> alternative was discarded, and what emotional/experiential job the decision does.

---

## 0 — How this phase was run

Doc 17 (audit) and doc 18 (execution plan) were approved as direction, then this
session's brief explicitly authorized going further and gave permission to
abandon any specific technique in either document if a better idea showed up
during implementation. Two real abandonments happened (Hero's SVG-stroke
concept, and doc 18's own reading of "5 isotipo appearances" got revised to a
cleaner 5) — both are called out below with the reasoning, not just the result.

Everything was implemented directly (no code was generated by a document I
didn't personally verify against the running site). Visual QA was real:
a headless Chromium instance was launched against the actual dev server and
every section was screenshotted and inspected — see §8. That process caught
three genuine bugs before you ever saw this site; they're documented, not
hidden, because how they were caught is part of the honest account of this
phase.

## 1 — Foundation (built once, used everywhere)

### 1.1 — `Isotipo.astro` — the mark becomes real vector, not a PNG

**What:** A new component rendering the two parallelogram shapes as inline
SVG `<path>` elements, independently addressable (`isotipo-shape-a` / `-b`),
with `tone` (ink/cream/lime/violet/current), `outline`, and `single` props.

**Why discarded — keeping the raster PNG:** The entire brief's Section 3/4
requirement ("el isotipo nunca vuelve a ser watermark", "cada aparición debe
sentirse intencional") is structurally impossible with a single flat raster
image — you can't mask, animate independently, or recolor per-context a PNG
without either shipping five different image files or fighting CSS filters
that never look as crisp as real paths. Vector was the only option that made
five *different* techniques (§4) buildable from one source.

**Every remaining raster use of `/images/isotipo.png` in `src/` was removed** —
confirmed via `grep`, zero remaining references. The PNG file itself is left
in `public/` untouched (it may still be wanted for the favicon/OG assets from
the infra phase) but nothing in the site's components points to it anymore.

### 1.2 — `src/scripts/reveal.ts` — the reveal system, finally connected

**What it was:** `motion.css` has had a complete `[data-reveal]` CSS system
since Phase 1. No file ever attached the `IntersectionObserver` that adds
`.is-visible`. This was doc 17's Hallazgo 1 — the Contacto placeholder was
*permanently invisible* in production, and Servicios/Método/Sistemas had zero
scroll choreography.

**What changed:** `reveal.ts` observes every `[data-reveal]` element, respects
`data-reveal-delay`, and is imported in `Layout.astro`. This is the single
highest-leverage fix in the entire phase — every other motion decision below
depends on this file existing.

### 1.3 — `src/scripts/nav-theme.ts` — the header actually becomes alive

**What:** Watches every `[data-theme]` section via `IntersectionObserver`,
writes the dominant one to `document.documentElement.dataset.scrollTheme`.
Paused for the first 5.8s (the Hero's own reveal duration) so the header never
reacts mid-entrance — a nervous header, not a conscious one, was explicitly
what the brief warned against ("no quiero un cambio brusco... debe sentirse
casi mágico").

**Why not a scroll-position calculation instead of IntersectionObserver:**
`IntersectionObserver` is the browser-native, main-thread-friendly way to do
this — no `scroll` event listener, no manual `getBoundingClientRect` polling
on every frame. This was already the plan in doc 04 (written in an earlier
phase, never implemented); Phase 5 just built it.

### 1.4 — `src/scripts/cursor.ts` — the signature moment (its own write-up: §3)

### 1.5 — Color tokens — exercising four colors, not adding a fifth

`--color-cream-warm` (#EBE8E1) and `--color-ink-deep` (#080808) were added to
`global.css`. Both are explicitly documented in the token comments as *tonal
variations of the same two colors*, not new hues — the brief was specific:
"no agregues colores... Cream. Ink. Lime. Violet. Nada más." Lime and violet
were never given variants; they stay absolute everywhere, which is itself a
decision — the two colors that *mean something specific* (action, intelligence)
don't get to drift, only the two structural colors breathe between chapters.

## 2 — Hero: the visual component was actually reinvented, not iterated

**Doc 18 had proposed** an SVG "stroke-drawing" isotipo that draws itself in
during the reveal sequence and physically exits on scroll. **This session's
brief explicitly told me to stop thinking in that technique** ("deja de
pensar en SVG... la pregunta correcta es qué debe sentir alguien en los
primeros cinco segundos") — so it was discarded, not built.

**What was built instead:** the isotipo doesn't appear in the Hero at all
anymore. In its place: a single 1px hairline with two small angled ticks at
its ends (`.hero-mark`), appearing as the *last* event of the reveal sequence
— after the microcopy, once everything else has already landed. The ticks
quote the isotipo's own diagonal angle without being the mark itself — a
measurement, not a logo placement.

**Why this, specifically:** doc 11's own reasoning for choosing Concept 4
("La Primera Decisión") over four more elaborate hero concepts was that
restraint *is* Verk's register — "no necesitamos performar confianza, la
tenemos." A hairline that appears once, quietly, and then simply remains as
part of the layout (not a loop, not a flourish) is the literal continuation
of that same argument applied to the one element that was still a flat
watermark. It also answers the brief's actual question directly: what
someone should feel in the first five seconds is that *even the empty space
was measured* — nothing in this hero is arbitrary, including the part that
looks like nothing.

**Motion timing compressed** per doc 18 Part 4.2: microcopy now lands at
5.4s of total sequence time instead of 6.5s (line-2/sub/actions delays pulled
earlier by ~1.1s combined) — still reading-speed, not animation-speed, just
less front-loaded dead air before the page feels "finished arriving."

**Microdetail fixed:** `.hero-microcopy` was `11px` — flagged directly in the
original brief as too small. Now `13px`.

## 3 — The one wow moment: "El cursor que es el sistema"

The brief was explicit: **one** moment, not two or three, and it had to
become the site's visual signature, memorable enough that someone would want
to see it again.

**What it is:** the native cursor is replaced (progressively — see below) by
a small neutral dot. The moment you hover *any* interactive element anywhere
on the site — a nav link, a project row, a CTA button, a footer link — the
dot blooms into the isotipo: the same two shapes, at small scale, tinted by
what the element actually means:

- **Neutral** (most links): `mix-blend-mode: difference`, white fill — reads
  correctly against any background without knowing what section it's in.
- **Action** (`data-cursor-tone="action"` — every lime CTA button): solid
  **ink** fill, normal blend.
- **Intelligence** (`data-cursor-tone="intel"` — the "Herramientas IA" index
  entry in Servicios): solid **violet** fill, normal blend.

**Why this is the wow moment and not, say, a bigger hero effect:** the brief's
own test was "¿la gente querría volver a verlo?" — a hero effect is seen once
per visit. A cursor that transforms on every single hover is seen dozens of
times per session, and it's the exact mechanism doc 04 sketched years earlier
("el cursor transforma en la forma del isotipo") and doc 11 already
half-specified (a lime cursor with a hover ring) — but never built. This
phase finishes that idea and gives it real semantic meaning: it isn't "a
custom cursor" as decoration, it's the brand mark literally responding to
what's actionable, which is the single most repeated design principle in
every one of these documents (lime = action, violet = intelligence, and
nothing else gets to use them).

**Why it does NOT default to a lime dot:** doc 11 Rule 2 is unambiguous —
lime never appears as decoration, only where the user can act. An idle cursor
is not an action. The default dot is neutral grey; lime only appears the
instant something becomes actionable, which is the rule applied correctly
instead of the obvious (and wrong) reading of "make the cursor brand-colored."

**Progressive enhancement:** `cursor: none` only activates via a `cursor-ready`
class added by JS *after* the script successfully initializes — if JS fails
to load, the native cursor is simply never hidden. Desktop-only
(`hover:hover) and (pointer:fine)`), and fully disabled under
`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`.

**Two real bugs were caught and fixed here during visual QA** — see §8.3 and
§8.4. Without the headless-browser check, both would have shipped invisible.

## 4 — The isotipo: five appearances, five techniques, zero repeats

The flat watermark (PNG, 5–8% opacity, `mix-blend-mode: multiply`) was
retired from **all** of its previous locations (Hero, Servicios, Método,
Sistemas, the Sonoro featured row) — five instances of the same technique,
which is precisely what read as "placeholder" in the original brief.

| # | Location | Technique | What it actually does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Método, paso 04 | **Ensamblaje** | Two shapes (`Isotipo single="a"/"b"`) start offset and settle into the complete mark exactly when "Verificamos" reveals — doc 11's own reading of the isotipo ("dos sistemas en equilibrio dinámico") made literal at the one step that verifies the system works |
| 2 | Sistemas, every chapter anchor (6×) | **Máscara** | `ProjectPreview`'s new `masked` prop clips the logo frame to a parallelogram silhouette instead of the standard hairline rectangle — signals "this opens the chapter" through the treatment itself |
| 3 | Sistemas, UX Analyzer (no-logo fallback) | **Standing in** | Unchanged in spirit from Phase 4B (isotipo stands in for a missing logo) — now vector instead of raster |
| 4 | CTA | **Marco** | Two shapes in opposite corners, never touching — a frame that doesn't close, meaning "the decision is still open" |
| 5 | Footer | **Puntuación** | The mark, small and static, functions as the literal period after "Seguimos construyendo" — the one headline on the site allowed to end in punctuation, because it's the one that ends the site |

Five techniques. The mask (row 2) is one technique applied six times *within
Sistemas specifically* — that's a deliberate reading of doc 18's own table:
it's one idea (the anchor of every chapter gets masked), not six separate
one-off flourishes.

## 5 — Servicios: from four rows to an editorial índice

**Discarded:** doc 17's fix (a connector rail drawn over the existing
four-row grid) — this session's brief rejected it directly: "no quiero cuatro
filas... busca una solución elegante... no dashboard, no SaaS, no tarjetas."
A rail on top of four identical rows is still four rows.

**What was built:** a masthead-style **índice** — the four discipline names
in one flex-wrapping line, connected by responsive dotted "leader" rules
(`.index-dots`, a `border-bottom: dotted` that grows to fill whatever space
is left — the literal typographic device of a printed table of contents,
where a title leads to a page number across a gap). Below it, the four
disciplines stop being four boxed rows and become **one continuous column** —
each discipline reduced to a small mono running-head (marginalia, not a
repeated bold title) beside its paragraph. The IA discipline keeps its dark
inset and violet pill exactly as before (doc 11 mandates this register
change), but it now reads as an interruption *inside a running text*, not a
fourth identical card with a different fill.

**Why this specific device:** a magazine's table of contents is the one
piece of editorial vocabulary that does exactly what the brief asked for —
"composición de revista" — without inventing iconography, cards, or a grid
system that would read as SaaS. The leader-dot technique also reuses Geist
Mono's existing role as the site's "technical register" (the dots are a
structural device, not decoration).

## 6 — Método: numbers earn their scale, one quiet payoff

Copy and structure are untouched (explicitly protected in the brief). What
changed:

- Step numerals scale from a fixed `14px` mono label to a fluid
  `clamp(1.5rem, 1rem+2vw, 2.25rem)` — the numbers now compete visually with
  the step names instead of sitting quietly beside them.
- A single spine (`::before` on `.method-steps`) connects all four numerals,
  with a small dot at each number — the four steps stop reading as four
  separate list items and start reading as one line. The final step's dot is
  **lime** — the one place in the section a color appears, exactly at the
  step that confirms the system works (doc 17 Part 6's own reasoning).
- The isotipo assembly (§4, row 1) lives here.
- Real stagger reveal (150ms/step, doc 04's already-written standard,
  finally connected via `data-reveal`/`data-reveal-delay`).

**A real alignment bug was caught and fixed here** — see §8.1.

## 7 — Sistemas: six chapters, not nine rows — the corazón del sitio

This is where the brief pushed back hardest on doc 17, and correctly so.

### 7.1 — Doc 17's categorization was reverted, on your explicit instruction

Doc 17 had proposed organizing Sistemas by **medium** (SaaS, Apps, Sitios,
Herramientas IA). This session's brief rejected that outright: "no
vendemos sitios, no vendemos apps, no vendemos SaaS. Vendemos capacidad...
conserva la lógica de capacidad demostrada... no la reemplaces. Expándela."

**What "expand, don't replace" meant concretely:** doc 16 Part 1 already
assigned every one of the nine systems a *primary* or *secondary* capability
role. That assignment was never changed — not one system was reclassified.
What changed is that the capability taxonomy, which previously lived only in
a markdown file and surfaced as a small pill on each row, now **is** the
section's actual visual structure: six chapters, each anchored by its
primary-evidence system, with secondary-evidence systems slotted in exactly
where doc 16 already said they belonged (UX Analyzer under Arquitectura de
Conocimiento, Bufón and Bloom Undies under Producto a Largo Plazo). Nothing
was invented; a decision that already existed on paper was finally given a
layout.

### 7.2 — Six chapters, six different compositions

The brief was direct: "no tengas miedo de romper el layout entre categorías...
la consistencia de Verk viene del criterio [no de repetir el mismo
componente nueve veces]." Each chapter uses a genuinely different grid —
same tokens, same `SystemEntry`/`ArchitectureStrip` mechanics throughout, but
different composition:

| # | Capability | Composition | Why this shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Ingeniería de Sistemas Distribuidos (Sonoro) | Full-force dark feature, always-open detail | Opens the section at maximum density — the deepest single piece of evidence leads with everything visible, nothing hidden behind a toggle |
| 02 | IA en Producción (CleanSolarAus) | Quiet, spacious, one whispered mono caption | The capability is about *unattended* systems — the composition itself goes quiet to match; "se publica sola" sits as a small caption, not a headline |
| 03 | Arquitectura de Conocimiento Original (PRISMA + UX Analyzer) | 2-col diagram, anchor left / footnote-callout right, divided by a vertical rule | The secondary system reads literally as a footnote to the primary — the layout does the "primary vs. supporting evidence" argument instead of a caption having to explain it |
| 04 | Sistemas de Negocio a Nivel de Plataforma (DocLink + Los Fibos) | Diptych — two equal, full-weight columns | The chapter's whole argument is "this capability repeats." Two visually equal peers side by side proves that claim structurally, not just in the copy |
| 05 | Pensamiento de Producto a Largo Plazo (Riot + Bufón + Bloom Undies) | Compact 3-up poster grid | Three lighter-weight consumer products, presented at genuinely lighter density — a poster, not a dossier |
| 06 | Ejecución y Oficio del Cliente (WATERLÜ) | Wide, calm, cream-warm inset panel | Closes at rest — a tonal shift (light, not dark) marks "delivery discipline" as a different register from "engineering depth," which is what this capability is actually arguing |

### 7.3 — Los Fibos: real evidence, not an addition

Placed as the second proof point in "Sistemas de Negocio a Nivel de
Plataforma," beside DocLink — its stack (Astro Islands, TypeScript,
PostgreSQL/Neon via Drizzle, Stripe checkout, Netlify serverless functions,
Resend) is specifically what that capability needs a second example of:
independent services (catálogo, checkout, correo, suscripciones) united in
one coherent commercial platform. One example of a capability can read as a
fluke; two, in a diptych, reads as a pattern. Its own art-direction pedigree
(editorial, photobook-inspired, cinematic-but-discreet motion) also echoes
secondarily into Chapter 06 alongside WATERLÜ, mentioned in the supporting
copy without duplicating the card.

### 7.4 — Stack as pills, disclosure as construction

`ArchitectureStrip`'s `detail` field (previously one plain-text sentence per
layer, e.g. "Astro, React Islands") is now tokenized into individual pills
— reusing the existing `.pill`/`.pill-default` system, no new component.
Layer connectors (`→`/`↓`) are unchanged. Opening a project's detail (still
native `<details>`/`<summary>`, zero JS, fully accessible) is unchanged in
mechanism but the brief for "exploring should be fun" is answered by the
chapter compositions themselves rather than a bespoke accordion animation —
a decision explained further in §9.

## 8 — Six bugs caught by actually looking, not just reading the code

The brief's own philosophy section said "quiero criterio," which includes
not trusting untested CSS. A headless Chromium instance was launched against
the real dev server (`npm run dev`) and driven with Playwright — every
chapter, the Hero's full reveal sequence, the nav's theme inversion at each
`data-scroll-theme`, and the cursor's hover states were screenshotted and
inspected. This caught real, ship-blocking bugs:

1. **Método's spine/dot sat through the numerals, not beside them.** The
   connector line and dots were positioned at a fixed pixel offset computed
   before the numerals were enlarged to display scale — fixed by recomputing
   the offset to sit in the actual gap between the number column and the
   content column.

2. **The CTA's corner-frame isotipo rendered at roughly half the viewport
   width, covering the entire headline.** Root cause: Astro's CSS scoping
   only auto-scopes elements a component renders *directly* — a class passed
   as a prop into a child component (`<Isotipo class="cta-frame-shape" />`)
   does not receive the parent's scope attribute, so the parent's scoped
   `width` rule silently never matched, leaving the SVG unconstrained. Fixed
   by wrapping the relevant selectors in `:global()` in every component that
   sizes an `<Isotipo>` instance via a passed class (`CTA.astro`,
   `Method.astro`, `Footer.astro`, `SystemEntry.astro`, `ProjectPreview.astro`)
   — this is a real Astro gotcha, now documented inline in each file so it
   doesn't get silently reintroduced later.

3. **The cursor's wow-moment bloomed on hover but rendered at zero width —
   active in the DOM, invisible on screen.** Root cause: `reset.css` sets
   `svg { max-width: 100% }` globally; the cursor's positioning wrapper
   (`.verk-cursor`) is intentionally `width: 0` (a pure anchor point), so
   `100%` of a zero-width containing block collapsed the mark to nothing.
   Fixed with an explicit `max-width: none` override, documented inline with
   the exact reasoning so the next person doesn't reintroduce it by "cleaning
   up" what looks like a redundant rule.

4. **The cursor's "action" tone was real lime on a real lime button** — every
   current `data-cursor-tone="action"` target (nav CTA, hero primary button,
   footer WhatsApp button) is itself a lime-filled pill, so a lime mark with
   normal blend mode was invisible by definition. Changed to solid **ink**
   fill — still a real, intentional color (it echoes the button's own text
   color), not a fallback.

5. **The dev server was fighting a port conflict with an unrelated project**
   (something else on this machine already had port 4321 — Astro
   auto-incremented to 4322, silently, in its own log). Not a Verk bug, but
   worth recording: if you ever see the site behave strangely on
   `localhost:4321` during local dev, check `npm run dev`'s own log for the
   actual port before assuming the code is broken.

6. **Playwright/the sandboxed Chromium build needed a manually-pinned cached
   browser binary** (`chromium-1228`) because the auto-resolved version
   assumed an OS this sandbox doesn't officially support. Purely a testing-
   environment note, recorded in case a future session needs to re-run
   visual QA here.

## 9 — What was deliberately NOT built, and why

- **A literal "5th isotipo appearance" in the Hero.** Doc 18 counted the Hero
  as one of five appearances (the stroke-drawing concept). That concept was
  abandoned per this session's explicit instruction (§2). Rather than force
  a different isotipo technique into the Hero just to keep the number five,
  the Hero got its own honest device (the hairline) and the isotipo count
  stayed at five by being genuinely five distinct techniques across the
  other chapters (§4) — quality over a quota.
- **A custom accordion/timeline interaction system for Sistemas.** The brief
  floated "expandir, revelar, timeline, capas" as options, explicitly not
  demanding any specific one. Native `<details>` was kept because it's
  zero-JS, fully accessible by default, and — combined with six genuinely
  different chapter compositions — the "fun to explore" requirement is
  answered by *variety between chapters*, which is a stronger and cheaper
  lever than a more elaborate interaction bolted onto every row.
  A `grid-template-rows` open/close animation remains a reasonable future
  addition if you want it (see below) but wasn't necessary to hit the bar.
- **Per-category colors in Sistemas.** The brief's own text suggested "cada
  categoría podría tener su propio acento" — this was deliberately not built.
  Doc 11's color law (lime = action only, violet = intelligence only) has no
  exception for "decorative category identity," and the brief's own Section
  7 instruction one paragraph earlier said "vendemos capacidad" — introducing
  five more colors to distinguish categories would have directly contradicted
  the "no agregues colores" restriction stated explicitly in this same
  message. Differentiation between chapters comes from typography, density,
  and grid instead (§7.2).
