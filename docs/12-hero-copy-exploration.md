# Verk — Hero Copy Exploration

> This document is not an implementation brief. It is not a component spec. It is not a motion guide.
> It is a search for the strongest possible language for a hero that relies entirely on language.
>
> The winning hero concept from `11-visual-identity-lock.md` is Concept 4 — **La Primera Decisión**.
> That concept has no video, no gradient, no simultaneous animation. It has a cream surface,
> an isotipo, and words that arrive one at a time.
>
> The copy is not supporting the design. The copy IS the design.

---

## Part 1 — Hero Intent

### What should visitors feel in the first 5 seconds?

**Calibrated surprise.**

Not the jolted surprise of something strange — the quieter surprise of something that is more considered than they expected. The digital agency market in León and Bajío trains business owners to expect a certain range of stimuli: bold colors, moving sliders, stock photos of handshakes, CTAs that read "¡Contáctanos hoy!" The expectation is low because the bar has been set low by every previous experience with a local or regional agency.

When a visitor arrives on the Verk hero — cream, clean, almost empty, the isotipo already there, the headline arriving one word at a time — the first feeling should be: *wait. This is different.* Not aggressively different. Not "trying to be different." Simply different, in the way that a well-made room is different from one that has too much furniture. The difference is in what was removed, not in what was added.

They should also feel, within three or four seconds, something like: *this is serious.* Not cold. Not corporate. Serious in the way a precise person is serious — the impression that whoever made this knows exactly what they're doing and is in no hurry to prove it.

### What should they understand immediately?

**That Verk builds systems, and that systems are not the same as websites.**

This distinction is the entire strategic claim of the brand. It should be undeniable within the first hero unit — not explained at length, but made clear enough that a clinic owner from León who has been burned by three previous agencies understands: this is categorically different from what I've tried before.

Specifically: visitors should understand that Verk connects something. Not just designs something. Not just codes something. Connects: their online presence to their actual operation. The before-state that the hero implicitly references is the disconnected business — leads arriving via WhatsApp and dying there, a site that looks acceptable but generates nothing, follow-up that depends on whether someone remembered to do it today.

The after-state that the hero implicitly promises is a business that runs on a digital layer that works without being watched.

### What should remain slightly mysterious?

**How Verk achieves it. And who, exactly, Verk is.**

The hero is not the place to explain four service categories with bullet points. It is not the place to show case studies. It is not the place to establish team credentials or years of experience.

The hero should leave the visitor with a specific curiosity: *how do they do this?* And a secondary curiosity: *who makes this?* The mystery is not confusion — it is the right kind of incompleteness. The kind that pulls you into the next section because you need to resolve it.

The isotipo already present on the page before the headline arrives contributes to this. The visitor's eye finds the shape before the text loads. There is a moment — brief but real — where the shape is the only element on the cream surface, and the shape alone communicates: there is an intelligence here, and it has been here longer than you have.

### What should make them think "this is not another agency"?

**Three things working simultaneously:**

First, **what the headline does not say**. It does not say "transformamos tu presencia digital." It does not say "soluciones a tu medida." It does not say "somos apasionados del diseño web." Every word that a competitor would use is absent. The absence is legible to anyone who has read agency copy before, which is everyone in the target market.

Second, **the pace**. In a world where hero sections arrive simultaneously — five animated elements appearing at once, video backgrounds playing before the page has finished loading — a hero that builds itself one element at a time at reading speed is genuinely unusual. It communicates: we are not rushing. The work does not need to perform. It simply arrives.

Third, **the specificity**. The language of Verk's hero references real things: leads, operations, systems, flows. Not metaphors of transformation. Not promises of growth. Specific, named things that a business owner recognizes as components of their actual working day. This specificity signals: the people who made this have been inside a real business. They understand the problem at the level of its mechanics, not its aspirations.

## Part 6 — Three Finalist Directions

Three finalists. Three different strategic angles. They do not overlap.

---

### Finalist A — The Structural Claim

**Combination 01**

> Construimos sistemas, no sitios.
> De la primera visita al cliente que paga — sin seguimiento manual, sin leads perdidos, sin plantillas.

**The idea:**

This headline works by creating an immediate, unmistakable distinction between what Verk does and what every competitor does. The visitor does not need to read further to understand the positioning. "Sistemas, no sitios" is a complete argument in four words.

The word "sistema" is doing significant work here. It is not abstract — in Spanish, "sistema" carries a concrete, operational connotation. A medical system. A filing system. Something that runs. "Sitio," by contrast, is immediately understood as a product that was delivered and left behind. The contrast is not between a good website and a bad website — it is between a different category of thing altogether.

The subheadline converts the structural claim into a specific operational promise. "De la primera visita al cliente que paga" is a complete customer journey in eight words. It names both endpoints without naming the middle — which is exactly where the curiosity is. How? That's what the rest of the page answers.

**Who it attracts:**

Business owners who have already hired an agency, received a website, and experienced zero measurable outcome from that investment. They are not cynical — they are specifically educated. They know what they don't want. This headline speaks directly to that knowledge and names the alternative without condescension.

Also attracts: business owners who have been meaning to "do something about the digital situation" for two years but haven't been convinced that any option available to them would be substantively different from the last one. This headline offers that differentiation in the first line.

**What it risks:**

Directness can read as aggression. "No sitios" might briefly alienate a visitor who arrived looking for a website and genuinely wants only a website. This risk is acceptable — that visitor is not Verk's client. The copy is filtering, not excluding.

The subheadline risks density. Three negative constructions ("sin seguimiento manual, sin leads perdidos, sin plantillas") in sequence can feel like a list of problems being defensively addressed. A visitor with high trust won't notice. A skeptical visitor might notice too much.

---

### Finalist B — The Operational Problem

**Combination 06**

> El prospecto de las 11pm también merece respuesta.
> Construimos el sistema que captura cada prospecto, automatiza el seguimiento y conecta con tu CRM — sin depender de que alguien recuerde hacerlo.

**The idea:**

This direction opens with a specific scenario — the 11pm prospect — rather than a structural claim. The 11pm is carefully chosen. It is not extreme enough to be absurd (3am would be funny, not credible). It is specific enough to be real. Every business owner who has missed a WhatsApp message because it arrived after hours will recognize it immediately.

The mechanism at work here is recognition before aspiration. Before selling the solution, this combination names the exact moment when the problem costs the business the most. A lead at 11pm, on a phone screen, is a lead that will be forgotten by morning or answered tomorrow at 9am when the prospect has already made a different decision. The headline does not explain any of this — it trusts the reader to complete the narrative from their own experience.

The subheadline is the most technically specific of the three finalists. It names three discrete functions — capture, automate, connect — and names the integration point (CRM). For the business owner who already uses a CRM and has never been able to connect it to their website properly, this is immediately credible.

**Who it attracts:**

Clinic owners who manage patient inquiries. Service businesses with high inquiry volume. Anyone who has watched a weekend of unattended WhatsApp messages become lost clients on Monday morning. This is the most emotionally resonant direction for the primary target market.

Also attracts: business owners who are not convinced by abstract claims about "systems" but are persuaded by concrete, recognizable scenarios. The 11pm specificity does more persuasive work than five minutes of sales conversation.

**What it risks:**

This direction ties the brand too specifically to automation and WhatsApp flows. Verk builds more than this — websites, AI tools, full digital product design — and a hero that emphasizes the automation angle may narrow the perception of what Verk can do. A visitor who arrives looking for a premium website for their architecture firm might not recognize themselves in "el prospecto de las 11pm."

There is also a small risk of tabloid register — "you're losing leads right now!" is a classic anxious agency move. This combination skirts that territory without crossing it, but the line is narrow.

---

### Finalist C — The Brand Statement

**Combination 03**

> La primera decisión ya fue tomada.
> El sistema que conecta tu presencia digital con tu operación real. Construido para funcionar sin que lo recuerdes.

**The idea:**

This direction is the most conceptually ambitious and the most aligned with the identity of the hero concept itself. "La primera decisión ya fue tomada" is a statement that exists at the intersection of infrastructure and creative philosophy. In the construction world, "la primera decisión" is the one that determines the entire obra — the choice of orientation, the position of the load-bearing wall, the decision about where the entrance faces. Once it is made, everything else follows.

In the hero context, "la primera decisión" is the decision to build a real system rather than a surface-level digital presence. The phrase implies: this decision was already made before you arrived. Verk is not presenting options. It already knows what the right thing to build is. The visitor is arriving at a studio that has already begun, not at a studio waiting to be hired.

The phrase also functions as a visual anchoring line for the hero concept. The isotipo is already present when the page loads — as if it has been there for some time. Then this headline arrives: "La primera decisión ya fue tomada." The isotipo IS the decision. The obra already exists.

**Who it attracts:**

Clients who resonate with the conceptual. Creative directors. Architects. Business owners with a developed aesthetic sensibility who are specifically looking for a studio partner, not a vendor. The founder of a startup who wants their digital infrastructure to feel like it belongs to the kind of company they intend to build, not the company they are today.

Also attracts visitors who are not immediately in need of a system but who are building a mental shortlist — who save the URL, who come back in three months when the moment is right. The headline is memorable enough to survive the delay.

**What it risks:**

Clarity is the cost of conceptual ambition. A clinic owner from León who arrives at this hero may not immediately understand what Verk is offering. "La primera decisión ya fue tomada" does not name the product, the service, the market, or the pain point. It requires the visitor to stay long enough for the subheadline to arrive — and if they don't, nothing was communicated.

This direction also has the highest execution dependency. If the hero motion is not built with the restraint and precision of the concept — if the headline "arrives" clumsily, if the timing is off, if the isotipo doesn't pre-exist the text — the phrase collapses. It becomes vague instead of deliberate. The words need the design to work. The design needs the words to matter.

## Part 7 — Audience Simulation

Five audiences. Three finalists. Fifteen brief scenarios.

---

### Audience 1 — Clinic Owner (Estética, Odontología, Dermatología — León)

**Budget:** ≈ $25,000–$80,000 MXN for digital infrastructure
**Current state:** Manages leads via WhatsApp groups. Has a Facebook page that was updated last in 2023. Had a website built two years ago that no one maintains. Books appointments by phone.
**Primary question:** "¿Esto me va a traer más pacientes?"

#### Finalist A: "Construimos sistemas, no sitios"

**What they understand:** Immediately — this is not another web design service. The "no sitios" lands as a category distinction.
**What attracts them:** The specificity of "sin leads perdidos" and "sin seguimiento manual" names their exact daily problem. They have lost leads. They have forgotten to follow up.
**What could confuse them:** "Sistema" in a tech context sounds expensive and complex. Does this work for a small clinic? The headline doesn't signal scale.

#### Finalist B: "El prospecto de las 11pm también merece respuesta"

**What they understand:** This scenario is their life. The missed WhatsApp. The unanswered form. They get this before they finish the sentence.
**What attracts them:** Complete recognition. The copy is describing their Tuesday evening. Trust is built at the headline level, before any product explanation.
**What could confuse them:** The subheadline names CRM — a term they may not use. "Conecta con tu CRM" might need a soft translation ("tu sistema de clientes") for this audience.

#### Finalist C: "La primera decisión ya fue tomada"

**What they understand:** Likely less than 50% on first read. The phrase is conceptually rich but not immediately operational.
**What attracts them:** If they stay for the subheadline, the "operación real" framing grounds them. If the motion is working correctly, the unhurried pace signals quality.
**What could confuse them:** "¿Qué decisión?" They don't have context for the philosophy. A skeptical clinic owner may click away before the anchor phrase finds its meaning.

### Audience 2 — Architect (Despacho de Arquitectura — Bajío)

**Budget:** ≈ $30,000–$120,000 MXN
**Current state:** Has a portfolio site built by a colleague's recommendation. Wants something that reflects the quality of their work. Doesn't fully trust "web agencies."
**Primary question:** "¿Van a entender lo que es mi despacho?"

#### Finalist A: "Construimos sistemas, no sitios"

**What they understand:** The architecture of the claim is familiar — they work with structural reasoning every day. "Sistemas" makes sense.
**What attracts them:** The precision of the language. Short sentences. No filler. They recognize craft in the writing.
**What could confuse them:** The subheadline's automation focus ("leads," "CRM") feels less relevant to a firm that closes projects via personal relationship, not inbound volume. They may feel this is for a different kind of business.

#### Finalist B: "El prospecto de las 11pm también merece respuesta"

**What they understand:** Less. An architect with a premium portfolio is not typically thinking about 11pm leads as their primary problem.
**What attracts them:** Operationally specific — but possibly not for their operation.
**What could confuse them:** This may read as "for clinics and service businesses, not for us." The copy's specificity works against it with this audience.

#### Finalist C: "La primera decisión ya fue tomada"

**What they understand:** More than any other audience. The phrase is architectural. "La primera decisión" is the preliminary concept, the parti, the foundational idea that governs everything else. An architect lives this.
**What attracts them:** The restraint of the design, the pace of the reveal, the cream surface — these are design decisions that signal Verk has aesthetic authority. The combination of good copy and good design is the proof that Verk "gets it."
**What could confuse them:** The subheadline needs to quickly establish Verk's commercial scope ("sistema," "operación real") before this audience assumes Verk is a branding studio only.

### Audience 3 — Construction Company Owner (Constructora — León/Bajío)

**Budget:** ≈ $50,000–$200,000 MXN
**Current state:** Has a serviceable site. Needs lead capture for real estate projects and property listings. Loses leads to competitors with faster response systems.
**Primary question:** "¿Me va a traer más prospectos calificados?"

#### Finalist A: "Construimos sistemas, no sitios"

**What they understand:** "Construimos" resonates immediately — this is their vocabulary. The language is from the construction world, which creates immediate rapport.
**What attracts them:** The contrast with agencies they've already tried. "Sin leads perdidos" is exactly the metric they care about.
**What could confuse them:** Very little. This combination works exceptionally well for this audience. The vocabulary is shared.

## Part 8 — Winner

### The Winner Is Finalist A

> **Headline:** Construimos sistemas, no sitios.
> **Subheadline:** De la primera visita al cliente que paga — sin seguimiento manual, sin leads perdidos, sin plantillas.

### The Defense

**Finalist A wins on the metric that matters most at launch: communicating what Verk is to the widest relevant audience without losing the audiences that matter.**

Let's be precise about what "relevant audience" means. Verk's V1 market is León and Bajío. The primary clients are clinic owners, constructoras, despachos, and service businesses. These clients are not necessarily familiar with the language of creative studios. They are not necessarily comfortable with ambiguity. They are making a business decision — and they need to understand, within the first hero unit, that Verk can solve their specific operational problem.

Finalist A gives them that, immediately.

The four words "construimos sistemas, no sitios" do three things simultaneously:

**First, they create category separation.** The visitor immediately knows this is not another web design service. This matters because the first question any business owner asks — consciously or not — when they visit a digital studio's website is: "Is this different from the last one?" Finalist A answers that question in the headline.

**Second, they use the right vocabulary.** "Sistema" is not a frightening technical word in Spanish — it is a specific, operational word that business owners use comfortably. A medical system. A management system. Something that runs. By choosing "sistema" over "plataforma" or "solución" or "presencia digital," the headline places Verk in the vocabulary of the client's working day, not the vocabulary of the agency world.

**Third, they set up the rest of the site.** Every subsequent section — services, process, projects — is now answering the question the headline posed: what is the system? How does it work? What does it cost? What does it produce? Finalist A opens a question that the rest of the page answers. This is how great headlines function.

### Why Finalist B Loses

Finalist B — "El prospecto de las 11pm" — is a great *conversion* headline. It would perform well in a targeted landing page for clinics or for a specific campaign. But as a *brand* hero, it narrows Verk to one customer type and one operational scenario. A construction company owner does not immediately see themselves in it. A creative director dismisses it. A startup founder wonders if this is really their studio.

More critically: Finalist B leads with the client's problem, not with Verk's solution. This framing — known in copywriting as "problem first" — is effective for high-intent, specific-keyword traffic. It is less effective for brand discovery, which is what the hero serves. A visitor arriving cold at verk.mx is not searching for "11pm lead response automation." They are exploring whether Verk could be the right studio for them. Finalist A serves that exploration. Finalist B is already in the middle of a sales conversation.

### Why Finalist C Loses — And Why It Will Return

Finalist C — "La primera decisión ya fue tomada" — is the most beautiful headline in this document. It is also the most deeply aligned with the brand philosophy, the hero concept name, and the visual identity of the page it will appear on. There is a version of the future where this headline is the Verk hero headline.

That version is not V1.

The argument against Finalist C at launch is not that it is wrong — it is that it requires too much from a visitor who has not yet been given anything. Verk is not yet a known studio. It does not have the cultural presence that allows a concept-led headline to do its work unaided. When a visitor arrives cold, with no referral, no prior knowledge, no context — "La primera decisión ya fue tomada" asks them to invest in a mystery before they have any reason to trust the mystery.

At V2 — when Verk has a portfolio, a reputation, inbound traffic from referrals, and an audience that already knows what Verk is — this headline becomes available. It earns the right to be abstract when the brand has first established what it concretely does.

The decision to use Finalist A now is not a failure of ambition. It is the recognition that clarity is the foundation on which concept can eventually be built.
